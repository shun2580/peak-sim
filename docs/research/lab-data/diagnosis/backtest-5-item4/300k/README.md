# 300,000 rows: the steps of B, tabulated for the reading of the miss of the limit rps

Step-by-step tables for the 300,000-row ladder of B (the third round of the lab backtest, `docs/research/lab-backtest-3.md`), made from the records of that round. This round was **undecidable** (not a pass and not a fail); its step records are used here as records of what the database did. Nothing in this directory is a measurement, a prediction or a test, and nothing here changes a prediction, estimate, calibration, formula, rule, score or record. The 300,000-row count is the largest of the counts under study; the tool of that round was the one used before the changes of 2026-11-17 (see "Conditions").

| File | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `build_table.py` | the script; `python3 -I build_table.py` reads the records and writes the three CSV files beside it. It adds no number of its own. |
| `step_table.csv` | one row per step (30 steps: s-1 5, s-2 4, s-3 9, s-4 4, s-5 8): the columns of items 1 to 5 |
| `route_table.csv` | one row per step and route (240 rows): requests sent, the prediction's work per request, one-request time against the window, weight |
| `tail_table.csv` | the database container's CPU counter around the end of each step: the 30 s wait, the time up to the last sample, the rate before the first request of the step, the rate before the first request of the next step |

Records read (paths from the root of the repository): `poc/lab/predictions/ladder-prediction-3.json` (the committed prediction, commit `206d114`); `docs/research/lab-data/estimate-300k/300k/estimate.json` (its row estimate); `docs/research/lab-data/ladder-300k/s-N-300000/rung-NN-<rps>/` (`plan.json`, `run.json`, `record.json`, `summary.json`, `k6-output.txt`, `cpu.txt`).

## Conditions of this round (put at the head of each count, not mixed with the count)

- **Request time limit.** PHP `max_execution_time` 0, php-fpm `request_terminate_timeout` 0s, nginx `fastcgi_read_timeout` 600s, the load tool's time limit for one request 600 s (`settings.json` of every step; `ladder-300k/compose-B.diff`). No request ended by a time limit.
- **End-of-step wait: 30 s, with a cut.** The load tool of this round (`poc/lab/load/scenario.js` before commit `66d341e` of 2026-11-17) did not set `gracefulStop`, so k6's default of 30 s applied: a request still running 30 s after the scenario's end was cut by the tool (`k6-output.txt` ends with `N interrupted iterations`, at the plan's length + 30 s). The record has **no `end_of_step` field** and no count of requests sent. Later rounds wait as long as the request time limit.
- **The cut does not stop the work.** The database kept working for the cut requests (below, "What the records show" 4 and 5).
- **Limits.** The database container: 2 CPUs, `cpuset` 0-1 (`poc/lab/compose/lab.compose.yaml`); `innodb_buffer_pool_size` 2G; no memory limit written for the database. The app side (php-fpm and nginx): 6 CPUs, `cpuset` 2-7, **memory limit 6 GiB**, PHP `memory_limit` 4G. The load tool: 2 CPUs, `cpuset` 8-9. The memory guard stopped s-5 in step 9 (`check-oom`, exit 19); step 9 has no record and is not in these tables.
- **The count of requests (old rule).** The tool counted the requests that had *finished* in the window; `rps_valid`, `rps_status` and the stop reason `rps_not_reached` of the records follow that count (spec 7.4.3 changed it later to the requests *sent*). The three steps flagged not valid (s-1 step 5, s-2 step 4, s-4 step 4) each have 37 of the 40 requests planned for the window completed and 0 dropped iterations; the missing are cut ZIP requests. This is why the sent counts here (from the plan) differ from `requests_in_window` of the records.
- **Why the round is undecidable** (`lab-backtest-3.md` sections 4.3 to 5.4): the limit rps could be measured for 1 scenario (s-3) of the 4 needed. The records show the cut ZIP requests above, and `GET /books/{slug}` answered HTTP 500 in **every request of every window** (SQLSTATE 1390, too many placeholders), so the measured utilization lacks the database work that page would have done after the error while the prediction counts it to its end (the page's share of the predicted work is 3.34% in s-1, s-2 and s-3, 0.86% in s-4 and 12.13% in s-5; `route_table.csv`).
- **The database's own work is not otherwise disturbed:** no ANALYZE ran during B; the idle rate of the database container is about 0.01 to 0.02 core (`tail_table.csv`, steps with an empty tail).

## Scales (what the percent is of)

- **Predicted utilization** (`pred_util_pct`): `scenarios[].stages[].utilization.point` of the committed prediction for the step's total rps, times 100. It is the sum over routes of (rps of the route × the prediction's database time of one request) divided by 2 cores, a rate, with the database time of one request = `c_q` × statements + `c_row` × rows of the estimate (`c_q` 2.8441e-4, `c_row` 9.3796e-7; the script checks that the prediction's per-request values equal this from the estimate: deviation 0). The point side is used; the upper side is only in `req_time_s` (below).
- **Measured utilization** (`meas_util_pct`): `db_utilization_percent` of the step record = the database container's CPU time over the window (`usage_usec` counter, `cpu.txt`) ÷ (2 CPUs × window length). The script rebuilds the window's CPU time from `cpu.txt` by linear interpolation at the window's two ends and gets the record's value to 3e-12 s in every step (`window_cpu_s_from_counter` against `window_cpu_s_in_record`, `tail_table.csv`).
- Both scales are the same (database CPU time over 2 cores × window), so `diff_pt` = predicted − measured is in points of that scale. Both are of the container's counter; neither is a share of one request's time.
- `pred_work_cpu_s` = Σ sent requests × work per request, in CPU seconds; `pred_work_cpu_s` ÷ (2 × window) differs from `pred_util_pct` by at most 0.152 point (the plan's counts are whole numbers).

## Columns, and where each comes from

`step_table.csv`

1. `pred_util_pct`, `meas_util_pct`, `diff_pt`: above.
2. `sent_requests`: the plan's `planned_window_requests` summed over routes, **not a count made by the tool** (it did not count sends). It is exact under two checks that pass in all 30 steps: dropped iterations are 0 in the record, and the arrival schedule rebuilt from the plan (first arrival at the stream's start, evenly spaced after it; the first minute is the discarded start) gives the same count in the window as the plan's. `pred_work_cpu_s`, and `route_table.csv` `sent_requests`, `pred_work_per_req_cpu_s`, `pred_work_cpu_s`: the prediction's one-request database time (point side) × the sent count. The ZIP export is 96.6% of the predicted work in s-1, s-2 and s-3, 99.1% in s-4 and 87.8% in s-5.
3. `after_window_requests`: requests still running at the end of the scenario, read from the load tool's progress lines (once per second, per route, the number of busy VUs; `k6-output.txt`). It counts requests of all phases: a request sent in the discarded first minute and still running is in it, and the tool cannot tell it from one sent in the window (at most one per route could be so, the stream's first arrival; for the ZIP stream that is the arrival at the stream's start). `max_end_of_step_delay_s`: the last second at which any request was still running, minus the end of the scenario, to the nearest second; **`>=30`** means the 30 s wait ended before the request did (the tool's own last line says `N interrupted`), so the delay itself is not in the record; a number is a delay seen (steps with 0 or a short delay).
   `spill_work_cpu_s`: the DB CPU **counter difference between the window's end and 30 s later** (the wait), all requests together, **given only for the 6 steps in which the tool cut no request** (s-3 and s-5 at 0.025, 0.035 and 0.05 rps; 0.5 to 7.9 CPU s; not net of idle, 0.02 core = 0.6 s in 30 s). **NA for the 24 steps in which the tool cut running requests** (`interrupted_at_cut` of 1 or more): the work of those requests goes on after +30 s (4 below) and cannot be separated from the next step's own work; `spill_missing` says what is missing. For those 24 steps the counter of the wait is in `tail_table.csv` (`tail30_cpu_s`) and the counter up to the last sample, 54 to 59 s after the end, in `to_last_sample_cpu_s`; both are **lower bounds** of the work after the window, not estimates. **No per-request CPU time or progress is in the record**, so the work of requests started in the window and done after it cannot be split from (i) requests sent in the discarded minute, (ii) requests left by the step before (5 below).
4. `weighted_req_over_window`, `req_over_window` (`route_table.csv`): `req_time_s` ÷ the window length, with `req_time_s` = the prediction's `php_cpu_bound_s` (the upper bound of the PHP CPU time of one request, the same quantity as the 540.9 s of `lab-backtest-5.md`; at 300,000 rows 954.6 s for the ZIP export, 36.4 s for the book page, under 1 s for the others), `req_time_source` = `upper_bound`. The weight is the route's share of the step's rps (`plan.json` `share`, summing to 1); the weighted value is Σ weight × `req_over_window`. A measured per-route request time is not in the step records; see "What the records show" 6. The same ratio on the database time (the point side: 104.75 s for the ZIP export, 3.616 s for the book page) is 0.065 (window 1,600 s) to 0.294 (window 356 s) for the ZIP export and 0.002 to 0.010 for the book page; this is not in the CSV.
5. `queue_sign`, `queue_evidence`: **yes** if some route had two or more requests running at the end of the scenario (later arrivals started while earlier ones were still running, and had not finished at the end); **no** otherwise; `NA` if the progress lines are missing (none is). `queue_evidence` gives, per route with a request running at the end, the number at the end, the highest number in the window and the first second at which two or more ran, then the DB CPU rate of the window's last 60 s against the whole window, and the longest completed request of the step (`summary.json`; the route is not recorded). The record holds no per-request delay over time (no per-request log), so "the delay grows within the step" is read only from the number of requests stacking.

## What the records show (facts; and, marked, what is consistent with them but not shown)

1. **Low steps: the predicted and measured utilization are close, and the sign varies.** At a predicted utilization under 40% (17 steps) the differences run from −1.5 to +7.2 points, 10 of the 17 within ±2.5; the steps of s-3 and s-5 at 0.1 rps (window 400 s) are at +6.7 and +7.2. (Numbers from `step_table.csv`; this directory does not read them for a cause.)
2. **Windows have 40, 80, 120 or 160 requests**, because the tool makes the window a multiple of 40 ÷ rps seconds; a step at 0.025 to 0.1 rps sends 40 requests whatever its rps, so `pred_work_cpu_s` is the same across those steps of a scenario and `pred_util_pct` rises only as the window shortens (s-1: 541.9 CPU s in 1,600 s down to 400 s).
3. **Requests were still running at the end of the scenario in 26 of 30 steps**, from 1 to 8 (`after_window_requests`); in 24 of those the delay is `>=30` (the other two, s-3 and s-5 at 0.05 rps, ended 16.8 s after the end; the four steps with none are s-3 and s-5 at 0.025 and 0.035 rps). The requests running at the end are mostly ZIP exports; the book page is among them in 9 steps (1 to 3 of it). The DB CPU in the 30 s wait (`tail30_cpu_s`) is 0.5 to 0.6 CPU s in the four steps with none running (0.2% of the step's window CPU time), 7.6 and 7.9 CPU s in the two with a 16.8 s delay (3.2% and 3.6%), and **14.5 to 58.5 CPU s in the 24 steps with cut requests, 1.8% (s-2 0.025) to 9.5% (s-3 0.45) of the step's window CPU time** (`tail30_cpu_s` against `window_cpu_s_from_counter`, `tail_table.csv`; lower bounds of the work after the window for those 24). At the highest step, s-3 0.45, the wait's 58.5 CPU s are at a rate of 1.95 cores, the same as the last 60 s of its window (1.95).
4. **The database was still busy for the cut requests after the tool stopped waiting.** The counter goes on to the last sample, 54 to 59 s after the end. In the 24 steps in which the tool cut one or more requests, the DB CPU rate in the last interval (`last_interval_rate_cores`) is 0.48 to 0.51 core in 12 steps (1 cut in each), 0.95 to 0.97 in 6 (2 or 3 cut), 1.32 in 3 (3 cut), 0.50 in 1 (2 cut), 1.62 (5 cut) and 1.92 (7 cut): it takes values near 0.485 core times a small whole number, and the larger the number cut the higher it is, with an exception (a step with 2 cut at 0.50) where one of the requests ended before the last sample. The rate in the 20 s after the wait (`post_wait_rate_cores`, +30 to +50 s) is within 0.03 core of the rate in the wait (`tail30_rate_cores`) in 15 of those 24 steps, and in the other 9 it is lower by 0.04 to 0.47 core (a request ended after the wait began or before it ended); in none does it fall to idle at +30 s. *Consistent with, not shown:* the steps of about 0.485 core are one ZIP export each (the only route long enough to be running 30 s after the end, apart from the book page); the record has no per-request CPU time.
5. **The work left by a step is present before the next step's first request.** The DB CPU rate in the sample interval before the first request of the step (`pre_start_rate_cores`) is 0.4 core or more in **20 of the 30 steps**; in 23 of the 25 pairs of consecutive steps of a scenario it is within 0.1 core of the rate in the last interval of the step before (`last_interval_rate_cores`), the two exceptions being s-1 step 1 to 2 (0.489 to 0.037) and s-1 step 4 to 5 (0.966 to 0.506), where some of the cut requests ended in the gap between the steps. The first step of s-2 and of s-3 began 3 s and 5 s after the previous scenario's last step (`chain.log`) at 0.96 and 0.97 core; the first step of s-1, s-4 and s-5 began at 0.02 core (a new start, a recreation of the application and web containers, or a hold). The CPU time of this left-over work **that falls inside a step's window is not separable** from the step's own work in the counter (the same container, no per-request CPU time); only its rate at the start is. It is therefore not in `tail30_cpu_s` or `spill_work_cpu_s` (which are about the work after the window) and not in any estimate here. This is the direction opposite to the spill of the wait: work of the step before is *added* to the measured utilization of the next step's window. How much it adds is not shown by these records.
6. **One ZIP request's time, consistent with the prediction but not shown route by route.** The longest completed request of a step (`summary.json`, all routes together) is 217.0 to 222.0 s in the lowest steps (s-3 and s-5 at 0.025 to 0.1 rps, s-1 0.025), and 218.9 to 308.5 s in the others (the 308.5 s of s-3 0.45). At one ZIP running the DB CPU rate is about 0.49 core. 217 s × 0.49 core is 106 CPU s, close to the prediction's 104.75 s for the ZIP export's database work. This is an inference (the record does not name the route of the longest request, nor give a ZIP's CPU time): it is *consistent with* the prediction's per-request value, not a measurement of it. A ZIP request of about 217 s is 0.14 of the 1,600 s window and 0.61 of the 356 s window.
7. **Steps with requests stacking** (`queue_sign` yes, 15 of 30): in s-3 0.45 seven ZIP exports were still running at the end, of eight sent in the window and one sent in the first minute; only 2 of the 8 had completed in the window (`run.json`: 2 of 8 planned), and the first second at which two ZIP exports ran together is 83 s into the scenario (`queue_evidence`). The 15 steps are those from 0.07 rps in s-1, from 0.035 in s-2 and s-4, and from 0.15 in s-3 and s-5.

## What is not in the records (NA, and why)

- `spill_work_cpu_s`: **NA in 24 of 30 steps** (the tool cut running requests, whose work goes on after +30 s and into the next step), with the missing items in `spill_missing`: the CPU time of each request, the split of the wait's work into this step's window requests, its first-minute requests and the step before's left-over, and the work after +30 s of the cut requests. The counter of the wait and to the last sample (`tail_table.csv`) is the lower bound the record gives. In the other 6 steps the value is the counter of the wait.
- A **measured one-request time per route**: not in the step records (`summary.json` has the average, 95th percentile and maximum of completed requests over all routes and no per-route time); `req_time_source` is `upper_bound` in every row.
- The **end-of-step delay** of the cut requests: only `>=30`.
- The **CPU time of the left-over work inside a window** (5 above).
- Requests *sent* are not a count made by the tool; see 2 above.

## Checks the script makes (printed when it runs)

- the prediction's database time of one request equals `c_q` × statements + `c_row` × rows of the estimate for the 8 routes (deviation 0), and is the same in the five scenarios;
- the window CPU time rebuilt from `cpu.txt` equals the record's `delta_usage_usec` in all 30 steps (largest difference 3e-12 s);
- for all 240 plan routes, the schedule rebuilt from the plan gives the plan's window count (mismatches 0), the plan's count equals `planned_requests` of `run.json` (mismatches 0), and dropped iterations are 0 (steps with a non-zero count: 0);
- `pred_work_cpu_s` ÷ (2 × window) against `pred_util_pct`: largest difference 0.152 point.

## What this does not do

It does not split the difference between predicted and measured into causes, and it does not say which of the effects above (the work of cut requests after the window, the work left by the step before, the failed book page, a request's own error) accounts for how much of it. It does not compare the three counts (90,000, 170,000, 300,000). No time, count or rate here is a prediction.
